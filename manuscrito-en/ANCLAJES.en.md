# Anchors — strings fixed before any chapter is translated

These are the strings that **repeat across chapters**. They are translated once, here, out of order,
and the chapters are then written *around* them. Every drift defect found in the Spanish original
was one of these losing its shape between one chapter and another.

Copy from here. Do not re-translate on the fly.

## 2 · The refrain

Bold, at the close of chapters 1 and 4. **Identical in both. Never paraphrased.**

> **If they sat down tomorrow to talk about you, what would they have on that table besides your
> numbers and your boss's opinion?**

And its answer, also in both:

> Almost always there are two things. Your numbers and whatever your boss thinks.

## 5 · The two triads — the pair that already collided once

The whole point is that these are **different questions**, and chapter 4 says so explicitly.

**Chapter 1 — the three criteria. About *a person*:**

> **capability, ambition and commitment.** That you can, that you want to, and that you're staying.

**Chapter 4 — the three gates. About *the situation*:**

> **demonstrated capability · an organizational opening · sponsorship**
>
> A candidacy doesn't move through a single chain. It needs **three gates to open at once**.

Never gloss one with the other's terms. `TRIADAS` checks this; it exists because chapter 12 once
said "the three criteria: that you can, that there's room, and that you want to" — a hybrid that
appears nowhere.

## 7 · The five tanks (chapter 5)

Each is defined as *fills with X; drains with Y*. The pairing is the whole device.

| Spanish | **English** | fills | drains |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capacidad | **Capability** | doing hard things with someone watching | on its own, with the years |
| Evidencia | **Evidence** | writing it down and teaching it | when your boss changes, when you change company, when enough time passes |
| Confianza | **Trust** | delivering what you said, in small repeated windows | very fast with one broken promise, very slowly with silence |
| Opciones | **Options** | keeping contacts alive, knowing what the market pays | by default, without you doing anything |
| Energía | **Energy** | sleeping, and work that goes well | wear, the sense of not moving, work that consumes and leaves nothing |

And the section that follows them, which must not claim an exclusive that the definitions
contradict — `DEPÓSITOS` checks this:

> **The tank nobody refills for you.** The other four refill themselves, at least partly, while you
> do your job. Options don't. It drains on its own, like capability; the difference is the other
> half — capability goes back up while you work, and options don't.

## 9 · The scale of what you can ask for (chapter 12)

> In that conversation **you do not ask for a promotion.**

Ordered easiest to hardest to grant:

1. That he knows where you want to go.
2. That he tells you what you're missing, specifically, with the names of things, not adjectives.
3. An assignment at the next level, with the email from chapter seven.
4. That he introduces you to someone, or takes you into a room you don't normally enter.
5. That he backs you when the moment comes.

And the sentence to learn word for word:

> *If the answer today is no, what would have to be true for it to be yes, and when do we review it?*
