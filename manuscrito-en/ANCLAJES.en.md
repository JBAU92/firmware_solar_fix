# Anchors — strings fixed before any chapter is translated

Strings that **repeat across chapters**, translated once, out of order. Chapters are then written
*around* them. Every drift defect found in the Spanish original was one of these losing its shape
between one chapter and another.

Copy from here. Do not re-translate on the fly.

> **Revision 2.** Reviewed against the original line by line before any prose was translated. Six
> meaning errors and three wrong pointers were found and are fixed below. The arithmetic was clean
> in revision 1 and still is: all seven ordinals, all seven remainders, all seven chapter numbers.

## 2 · The refrain

Bold, in chapters 1 and 4. **The Spanish had drifted here** — chapter 1 said *"la opinión de tu
jefe"* while chapter 4 and the answer line six lines above it said *"lo que opine tu jefe"*. The
original has been unified; English follows the colloquial form, which is also the one that matches
the answer and the chapter-12 close.

> **If they sat down tomorrow to talk about you, what would they have on that table besides your
> numbers and whatever your boss thinks?**

The answer, which appears in the prologue (third person), chapter 1 and chapter 4:

> Almost always, it's two things. Your numbers, and whatever your boss thinks.

## 3 · The running tally

**This section fixes the tally line only** — the ordinal, the remainder and the gloss. The rest of
each chapter's closing paragraph is translated with its chapter, and carries content these lines
deliberately compress.

| ch. | **English** |
|---|---|
| 6 | That's the first of the seven you can put on that table, and it's the cheapest of them all. **Six to go.** |
| 7 | Second of the seven on the table: one thing at the level above, start to finish, with a result you can tell someone about. **Five to go.** |
| 8 | Third on the table, and the first that doesn't depend on you alone. The first two were proof you can make yourself; this one is a reputation. **The last four all work like that.** |
| 9 | Fourth on the table, and the first one that's a person instead of a piece of paper: someone willing to say your name in a room you're not in. **Three to go.** |
| 10 | Fifth on the table: people outside your own department who have one specific sentence they could say about you. **Two to go, and they're the big ones.** |
| 11 | Sixth on the table, and the hardest of the seven to argue with. **One to go.** |
| 12 | **And that's seven.** That table no longer has just your numbers and whatever your boss thinks on it. |

Fixes from revision 1: **8** — *"proof you manufacture"* reads in English as **falsified evidence**,
in the chapter whose whole point is that Jason did nothing dirty. **9** — *"isn't a document but a
person"* was a calque of *no es X sino Y*. **7** — *bounded* is math and software register, not a
book that talks to a kitchen; and "un resultado **que se puede contar**" is the chapter's hinge
(`07:193`), so *tell* has to survive. **10** — *your area* and *say one specific sentence* were both
calques. **12** — *decides about you* and *no longer holds only* were calques.

prose but do not reproduce the rows.

| | **what goes on the table** | ch. |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your impact, in a form someone else can repeat without you there | 6 |
| 2 | One thing at the level above, finished, with a result | 7 |
| 3 | People want to work with you | 8 |
| 4 | Someone credible defends you when you're not there | 9 |

## 5 · The two triads — the pair that already collided once

The whole point is that these are **different questions**, and chapter 4 says so explicitly.

**Chapter 1 — the three criteria. About *a person*:**

> **capability, aspiration and commitment.** That you can, that you want to, and that you're staying.

*Aspiration*, never *ambition*: it is the word in the model the book's own sources cite
(`16:27-29`, *ability / aspiration / engagement*), and *ambición* is a separate live word that
titles a section in chapter 3.

**Chapter 4 — the three gates. About *the situation*:**

> **demonstrated capability · an organizational slot · sponsorship**
>
> A candidacy doesn't move through a single chain. It needs **three gates to open at once**.

Gate 2's body: *"That there's a slot. A vacancy, budget, the right time of year."* Not *an opening*
— that word is reserved for actual job vacancies — and not *room*, which is the book's word for the
place you aren't.

Never gloss one triad with the other's terms. `TRIADAS` checks it.

## 6 · The five ladders (chapter 2)

> There are five ladders. Only one of them has a sign on it.

| Spanish (body / worksheet) | **English** |
|---|---|
| Mando | **Management** — running people and answering for what they do |
| Carrera experta / Experta | **Expert** |
| Movilidad lateral / Lateral | **Sideways** |
| Salida | **Out** — the same climb, in another building |
| Permanencia consciente / Quedarme | **Staying on purpose** |

Fixes: **Expert**, not *expert track* — the glossary bans *track* for this concept, and it was the
only one of the five carrying an extra noun. **Sideways**, because bare *Lateral* is not a noun in
English. **Staying on purpose**, because `02:52-57` says the adjective *consciente* is what names
the route, and the whole passage turns on the difference between choosing to stay and letting the
years pass — which *staying put* collapses.

## 7 · The five tanks (chapter 5)

| Spanish | **English** | fills | drains |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capacidad | **Capability** | doing hard things with someone watching | on its own, as the years go by |
| Evidencia | **Evidence** | writing it down and passing it on | when your boss changes, when you change jobs, when enough time passes |
| Confianza | **Trust** | small promises you keep, over and over | very fast with one broken promise, very slowly with silence |
| Opciones | **Options** | keeping contacts alive, knowing what the market pays | by default, without you doing anything |
| Energía | **Energy** | sleeping, and work that goes well | the grind, the feeling you're not going anywhere, work that eats the day and leaves nothing |

Fixes: **Evidence** — *enseñando* is not *teaching*. Chapter 6 says explicitly that training someone
once is invisible work and only *writing down how it's done* is evidence (`06:121`), so *passing it
on* covers both senses without contradicting it. **Trust** — *"in small repeated windows"* was
opaque and imported *window*, which chapter 4 uses for the promotion window; the Spanish says
*plazos*. **Capability** and **Energy** — calques.

And the section that follows, which must not claim an exclusive its own definitions contradict —
`DEPÓSITOS` checks this:

> **The tank nobody refills for you.** The other four refill themselves, at least partly, while you
> do your job. Options don't. It drains on its own, the way capability does. The difference is what
> happens on the other side: capability refills while you work. Options never do.

## 8 · The seven-line email (chapter 7), five items

Restated as a list in **chapters 12 and 13** (`12:120`, `13:84`) — not 11. Chapter 11's only
*carta* is at `11:130` and means **a restaurant menu**.

> **the goal · the measure · what you can decide · who's covering you · the end date**

The fourth item is *not* called **sponsor** here. Chapter 7 never uses *padrino* in Spanish —
`07:104` says "Quién responde por esto arriba. Alguien con peso" — and if English named it *sponsor*
in chapter 7, it would contradict chapter 9's tally line, which says the fourth thing on the table
is *the first one that's a person instead of a piece of paper*. From chapter 12 onward, once
chapter 9 has defined it, *sponsor* is correct.

## 9 · The scale of what you can ask for (chapter 12)

> In that conversation **you do not ask for a promotion.**

Easiest to hardest to grant. Second person, not five sentences opening *"That he…"* — which was
*"Que te…"* calqued, and which also made the whole scale male while the chapter's own example is
Marisol asking her nurse manager, who is a woman.

1. They know where you want to go.
2. They tell you what you're missing — specifically, with the names of things, not adjectives.
3. An assignment at the next level, with the seven-line email from chapter seven.
4. They introduce you to someone, or take you into a room you don't normally sit in.
5. They back you when the moment comes.

And the sentence to learn word for word:

> *If the answer today is no, what would have to change for it to be a yes — and on what date do we
> look at it again?*

**The date is not optional.** The paragraph right after it (`12:99-101`) says the third thing the
sentence does is *"pone una fecha, que es lo que impide que la conversación se evapore"*. Revision 1
said only *when*, which stops the sentence doing what the text says it does.
